## Beyond Incident Resolution: Why Major Incident Management is a C-Suite Imperative
Great perspective on the operational mechanics of Major Incident Management (MIM). 

Looking at this through a CTO and Boardroom lens, MIM isn't just an IT support function—it is the ultimate stress test of enterprise architecture, brand trust, and revenue protection.

When a P1 hits, the C-suite doesn't ask about ticket queues; they ask about business continuity, regulatory exposure, and customer churn. To elevate MIM from reactive firefighting to a strategic boardroom priority, technology leaders must shift focus upstream to Non-Functional Requirements (NFRs) engineered by design:

🔹 Security & Compliance:
Ensuring rapid war-room mobilization and recovery never bypass zero-trust guardrails or expose sensitive data. 

🔹 Resilience & Reliability:
Moving past basic uptime to self-healing architectures, chaos engineering, and strict SLO/Error Budget governance via SRE. 

🔹 Availability & Scalability:
Architecting platforms to absorb sudden demand spikes and regional failures without degrading customer experience.

The Next Frontier:
AIOps & Agentic AI Traditional ITSM integration connects the dots, but AIOps and Agentic AI close the loop at machine speed. Instead of waiting for human triage, autonomous AI agents—governed by strict Human-in-the-Loop controls—can correlate cross-stack telemetry, predict cascade failures before impact, execute automated runbooks, and feed RCA insights directly back into CI/CD pipelines.

In the boardroom, operational excellence isn't measured by how fast we put out fires—it’s measured by how few fires ever spark because resilience is baked into the platform's DNA.

How is your leadership team bridging the gap between day-to-day Service Operations and architectural NFR governance?
